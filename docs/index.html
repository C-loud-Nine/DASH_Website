Overview video

TL;DR

One constraint cannot pin down two branches.

The problem

One constraint, two branches

Classifier-free guidance combines a conditional and an unconditional prediction. Distilling only their guided combination gives one equation per input for two unknown branch outputs, so a compressed student can drift along a line of equally good solutions, including one where the guidance gap vanishes.

The fix

Supervise both branches

DASH matches the student's conditional output to the teacher's conditional output and its unconditional output to the teacher's unconditional output, plus a light anchor to ground-truth noise. Each branch then has a unique target per input.

The evidence

The null line, measured

The analysis predicts that a composite student's branch errors keep the ratio (w−1)/w = 0.75 at w = 4. Measured: 0.77, 0.73 and 0.79 on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-64.

Calibration on this page means teacher-relative guidance-gap preservation: how well the student's gap ΔS = εyS − ε∅S reproduces the teacher's gap ΔT, measured by its magnitude ratio ρ = E‖ΔS‖ / E‖ΔT‖ and its direction cos(Δ). Both are needed: a gap can have the right size and point the wrong way.

Abstract

Abstract

Parameter compression of class-conditional diffusion models exposes a structural limitation in output-level distillation: supervising only the guided output leaves the two score branches non-identifiable, so the classifier-free guidance gap is not determined in the student. The zero-loss set admits degenerate solutions in which both branches collapse toward identical predictions, and guidance loses its effect at inference despite low training loss. For each fixed input the objective is exactly flat in a branch-output direction at every residual value, so the ambiguity is a property of the output-level objective rather than a poor local minimum. This paper introduces DASH, which supervises the conditional and unconditional branches independently. An anchor term regularises the conditional prediction toward ground-truth noise, and the teacher's final learned per-timestep curriculum transfers into the student as a frozen prior. Across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and class-conditional ImageNet-64, a more than 5× compressed student stays within four FID points of its teacher and recovers at least 89% of the teacher's guidance-gap magnitude, where no other two-branch baseline with defined calibration exceeds 82%. Ablation isolates unconditional supervision as the term that separates this formulation from output-level distillation. The account is quantitative: the null space fixes the ratio between a composite student's two branch errors, and the measured ratios approximately match that prediction on all three datasets.

The problem

Composite distillation cannot tell recovery from collapse.

A guided teacher predicts ε̃T = ε∅T + w(εyT − ε∅T). Composite distillation (here, a two-branch adaptation of guided distillation, Meng et al., CVPR 2023) trains the student to match that single guided output. Writing the branch residuals as δy = εyS − εyT and δ∅ = ε∅S − ε∅T, the composite loss is zero whenever

That is a line, not a point. It contains ideal recovery (δy = δ∅ = 0) and guidance collapse (εyS = ε∅S), and composite supervision gives them the same loss. The ambiguity holds at every residual value, so it does not depend on reaching zero loss. It is a statement about the emitted branch outputs at a fixed input, not about the shared network parameters.

Method

Give each branch its own target.

Dual-branch objective

For a sample (x0, y, t) with noised input xt and noise ε, the frozen teacher gives εyT and ε∅T; the student gives εyS (forced label) and ε∅S (null token).

Here λan = 0.1, and ε is the ground-truth noise used to corrupt x0, not a teacher prediction. Branch weights are symmetric (λim = λun = 1). With Lim = Lun = 0 the student must match both teacher branches, which rules out shifts and collapse whenever ΔT ≠ 0.

TIRT Transfer: a frozen, learned timestep weighting

The teacher is trained with TIRT, which multiplies the Min-SNR weight by a learned per-timestep factor σ(λt) and normalises to unit mean:

It is an empirical reweighting: at λt = 0 it reduces to Min-SNR up to a constant. DASH copies the teacher's final normalised weights into the student and excludes them from the optimiser. In the ablation, using frozen Min-SNR weights instead costs 2.60 / 2.67 FID, and relearning the weights from λ = 0 costs 3.84 / 3.94 FID (CIFAR-10 / CIFAR-100).

TAG: an inference-time guidance schedule

TAG changes the guidance weight across the sampling trajectory and leaves training untouched. It is shared by the teacher and every two-branch student, and it does not change the branch targets used in distillation.

    Limitations

    What this does not show yet.

    1. The full baseline sweep and the ablations are at CIFAR scale. ImageNet-64 (1000 classes, 64×64) confirms the main effect, composite against DASH and the removal of Lun.
    2. Latent diffusion with cross-attention conditioning and transformer backbones remain open.
    3. The residual offset tracks capacity: under distillation ρ rises with student width (0.86 → 0.91 → 0.94), while the teacher-free reference stays near 0.81 / 0.80 / 0.83.
    4. TIRT and TAG are evaluated under one teacher recipe; a standard Min-SNR teacher preserves the ordering, but one extra recipe does not establish generality.
